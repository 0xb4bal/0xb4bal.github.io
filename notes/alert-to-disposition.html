INVESTIGATION METHODOLOGY

SOC process · independently reconstructed · no employer data

From alert to defensible assessment

An alert is a starting point, not a conclusion. My repeatable approach separates what the logs show from what I infer, and turns investigation into an actionable handoff.

My triage sequence

  1. Frame the alert. Identify the alleged behaviour, involved entity, source, time window and what actually triggered the alert.
  2. Validate telemetry. Inspect parsed event fields, then raw payload when important context is missing. Check field interpretation and query time boundaries.
  3. Search historical context. Look backwards for the IOC, source, destination, account or host. Ask whether activity is isolated, repeated, or correlated with other events.
  4. Pivot and enrich. Relate entities and use external threat-intelligence sources to inform hypotheses, not to replace observed behaviour.
  5. Examine the outcome. Distinguish an attempted connection or blocked action from a confirmed successful action. Firewall actions and reset events require interpretation in context.
  6. Decide and communicate. Record observations, assessment, uncertainty, potential impact, recommended action, and whether further investigation or escalation is needed.

Example: Tor-associated inbound traffic

Consider a hypothetical alert for traffic from an IP reported as a Tor exit node. That association alone does not establish a compromise. I would confirm the destination service, relevant period, network-control response, whether a session was established, related requests and any historical pattern. If logs are incomplete or the observed outcome is ambiguous, the disposition must reflect that uncertainty and may require escalation.

Illustrative scenario only: This page does not recount any particular customer incident or disclose original detection logic, internal thresholds or real telemetry.

Handoff template

  1. Observation: What was recorded, by which type of log source, and when?
  2. Assessment: What behaviour does the evidence support, and what remains uncertain?
  3. Impact: Which asset or service could be affected, without assuming damage?
  4. Recommended next action: Close as documented, collect more evidence, or escalate according to the applicable process.

Why I use this approach

Reputation-only triage encourages premature conclusions. Historical searching, correlation and explicit connection-outcome checks make an assessment more defensible. Good documentation lets the next analyst continue without repeating the same work.

This is generic personal methodology reconstructed from experience, not an employer SOP. No actual logs, IP addresses, client identifiers, tickets or internal playbooks are reproduced.
